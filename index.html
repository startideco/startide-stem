The program

A practical STEM club, not a lecture series

Students learn by working with real tools, real constraints, and real questions. Activities will be selected based on available equipment, student readiness, and the needs of the first cohort.

Problem-solving

How we learn

Small groups, visible thinking

The first class is intentionally limited in size. That allows students to ask questions, receive individual help, and spend more time doing rather than waiting.

Activities emphasize curiosity, careful observation, respectful teamwork, and learning from things that do not work on the first try.

About the instructor

Technology experience brought into the classroom

The goal is not simply to use equipment. It is to help students understand systems, make thoughtful decisions, and become more confident problem-solvers.

Frequently asked questions

What families may want to know

Does my child need prior coding or robotics experience?

No. Activities will be introduced at an appropriate starting level, with room for more experienced students to go further.

Why is the first cohort small?

The pilot is intentionally limited to approximately 5–10 students so the instructor can provide hands-on support and refine the program carefully.

What grades may participate?

The initial program is expected to serve students in grades 3–8. Final eligibility will follow the school’s program announcement.

What equipment will students use?

Activities may include classroom robotics, visual coding tools, engineering materials, and other available STEM resources. Specific equipment may vary by session.

Where and when will the club meet?

Final dates, times, location, tuition, and registration instructions will be posted once confirmed with the school.
